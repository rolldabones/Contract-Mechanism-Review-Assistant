### Issue 1: Low and variable liability cap
- **Where:** (A) cl. 8: "shall not exceed the fees paid in the 6 months before the claim."
- **What the contract says:** (A) Each party's total liability is capped at fees paid in the 6 months before the claim, except for clause 9 liability.
- **Inference:** (B) Fees are payable 45 days after invoice (cl. 3), so a claim early in the term could face a very low or near-zero cap. (B) "Paid" rather than "payable" means withheld or disputed fees may reduce the cap. (B) Because the cap is mutual, it also limits Customer's own exposure, which has some value to Customer.
- **Why it matters:** (B) Supplier's exposure for data loss, security failure or serious non-performance may be small compared with Customer's potential losses.
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Seek a cap based on the greater of a fixed sum or 12 months' fees "paid or payable," and seek separate, higher caps or uncapped treatment for data protection, confidentiality and security breaches.

## 5. Issue List (Prioritised)

### Issue 1: Low and variable liability cap
- **Where:** (A) cl. 8: "shall not exceed the fees paid in the 6 months before the claim."
- **What the contract says:** (A) Each party's total liability is capped at fees paid in the 6 months before the claim, except for liability under clause 9.
- **Inference:**
  - (B) Fees fall due 45 days after invoice (cl. 3), so a claim early in the term could face a very low or near-zero cap.
  - (B) "Paid" rather than "payable" means withheld or disputed fees may reduce the cap.
  - (B) The cap is mutual, so it also limits Customer's own exposure.
- **Why it matters:** (B) Supplier's exposure for data loss, security failure or serious non-performance may be small compared with Customer's potential losses.
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Seek a cap set at the greater of a fixed sum or 12 months' fees "paid or payable." (C) Seek separate, higher caps or uncapped treatment for data, confidentiality and security breaches.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested
- **Confidence:** (B) High

### Issue 2: Supplier's right to use Customer Data
- **Where:** (A) cl. 10: "Supplier may use Customer Data in aggregated form to improve its products."
- **What the contract says:**
  - (A) Supplier may use Customer Data in aggregated form to improve its products.
  - (A) "Customer Data" means "all data supplied by Customer to Supplier" (cl. 1).
- **Inference:**
  - (B) "Aggregated" is undefined and does not necessarily mean anonymised or non-attributable.
  - (B) Customer's sales and demand data may be commercially sensitive, and its use could indirectly benefit other users of Supplier's products.
  - (B) The right has no stated end date and may continue after termination.
- **Why it matters:** (B) This creates competitive and confidentiality exposure. (B) Any data protection exposure is jurisdiction-dependent (governing law Not found).
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Delete the right. (C) Alternatively, limit it to irreversibly anonymised data that cannot identify Customer, its stores or individuals, with no disclosure to third parties and with the right ending at termination.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested
- **Confidence:** (B) High

### Issue 3: Loss-of-profit exclusion may remove the main forecasting-related loss

- **What the contract says:**

- **Inference:**

  - (B) Whether losses from defective forecasts count as "loss of profit" or as direct loss is jurisdiction-dependent (governing law Not found).
- **Why it matters:** (B) Most of the commercial harm from the service failing may fall into the excluded category.
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Carve indemnity, confidentiality and data breach out of the exclusion. (C) List recoverable direct losses, such as the cost of reprocessing data and of procuring replacement services.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested
- **Confidence:** (B) Med
