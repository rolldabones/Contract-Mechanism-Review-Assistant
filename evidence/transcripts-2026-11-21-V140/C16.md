## 5. Issue List (Prioritized)

### Issue 1: Low liability cap and exclusion of loss of profit
- **Where:**
  - (A) Cl. 8: "shall not exceed the fees paid in the 6 months before the claim."
  - (A) Cl. 8: "Neither party is liable for loss of profit or indirect loss."
- **What the contract says:**
  - (A) Except for clause 9 liability, each party's total liability is capped at fees paid in the 6 months before the claim.
  - (A) Loss of profit and indirect loss are excluded for both parties.
  - (A) No other carve-outs appear in the provided text.
- **Inference:**
  - (B) Payment is due 45 days after a valid invoice (cl. 3). A claim early in the term could therefore face a cap near zero, because "fees paid" may be very low at that point.
  - (B) "Total liability" alongside "6 months before the claim" is ambiguous where there are multiple claims.
  - (B) The exception for "liability under clause 9" grammatically attaches to the cap sentence. It is unclear whether the loss-of-profit exclusion also applies to the indemnity.
- **Why it matters:**
  - (B) Customer's most likely losses from defective forecasting are lost profit and stock costs. The exclusion may remove the main recovery route.
  - (B) The cap appears to have no carve-out for data breach, confidentiality breach or fraud.
  - (B) Jurisdiction-dependent: under English law, some liabilities may not be limitable, and the meaning of "indirect loss" is a matter of construction. Please confirm.
- **Proposed fix:**
  - (C) Raise the cap to a multiple of annual fees, using "paid or payable," with a fixed minimum floor.
  - (C) Carve out from the cap and exclusions: data protection, confidentiality, security breaches, wilful default, fraud, and liabilities that cannot be limited by law.
  - (C) Define recoverable direct losses, for example costs of re-procurement and of restoring data.
- **Optional draft language:** (C) Not requested.
