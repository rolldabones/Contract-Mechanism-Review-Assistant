## 5. Issue List (Prioritized)

### Issue 1: Low liability cap combined with a lost-profit exclusion
- **Where:** (A) Cl 8: *"shall not exceed the fees paid in the 6 months before the claim … Neither party is liable for loss of profit or indirect loss."*
- **What the contract says:** (A) The cap is fees *paid* in the 6 months before the claim. Liability under clause 9 is excepted. Loss of profit and indirect loss are excluded for both parties.
- **Inference:**
  - (B) Early in the term, or when Customer has withheld disputed payments, "fees paid" may be very low.
  - (B) Because payment is due 45 days after invoice, the cap may be close to nil in the first months.
  - (B) No carve-outs appear for data breach, confidentiality, gross negligence or fraud, so those exposures appear to fall within the cap.
  - (B) Whether the clause survives a statutory reasonableness test is jurisdiction-dependent and is not concluded here.
- **Why it matters:** (B) A forecasting failure or a data incident could cause losses far above six months of fees. Much of that loss may be characterised as lost profit.
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Seek:
  - a cap based on the greater of a fixed sum or 12 to 24 months of fees *paid or payable*;
  - a separate, higher cap for data and confidentiality breaches;
  - uncapped carve-outs for fraud, wilful default and any other categories that law prevents from being limited;
  - narrowing the lost-profit exclusion so that specified direct losses (for example, wasted expenditure, cost of re-procurement, and data restoration) remain recoverable.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested.
- **Confidence:** (A) High.

### Issue 2: Broad Supplier right to use Customer Data

- **What the contract says:** (A) Supplier may use all data supplied by Customer, in aggregated form, to improve its products. No limits on duration, anonymisation or confidentiality appear in the provided text.
- **Inference:**
  - (B) "Aggregated" does not necessarily mean anonymised or non-attributable.
  - (B) Store-level sales data may be commercially sensitive, and improved products may benefit Customer's competitors.
  - (B) The right is not stated to end on termination.
  - (B) If Customer Data includes personal data, data-protection law may restrict this use. That point is jurisdiction-dependent and fact-dependent.
- **Why it matters:** (B) It creates competitive leakage and regulatory exposure, and no protective controls appear in the provided text.
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Seek:
  - restriction of the use to data that is anonymised and aggregated across multiple customers, so it cannot identify Customer, its stores or individuals;
  - exclusion of personal data;
  - a confidentiality obligation over the underlying raw data;
  - a prohibition on any use of the data for competitors;
  - an end to the right on termination, except for data that has already been irreversibly anonymised.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested.
