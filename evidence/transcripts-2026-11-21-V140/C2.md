## 5. Issue List (Prioritized)

### Issue 1: Low liability cap with no carve-out for data or confidentiality breaches

- **Where:** (A) Cl. 8: "shall not exceed the fees paid in the 6 months before the claim."
- **What the contract says:** (A) Each party's total liability is capped at fees paid in the 6 months before the claim. The only stated exception is "liability under clause 9."
- **Inference:**
  - (B) The cap depends on fees *paid*, so it could be low early in the term or during a payment dispute.
  - (B) "Total liability" may operate as a single aggregate cap across all claims.
  - (B) A data breach or misuse of Customer Data would fall within the cap.
- **Why it matters:** (B) The Customer's most serious exposures are data misuse and failed forecasts. Both could exceed six months' fees by a wide margin.
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Raise the cap to the greater of 12 months' fees *paid or payable* or a fixed amount. Add carve-outs or a separate higher cap for breach of data, confidentiality and security obligations. Add a carve-out for fraud and wilful misconduct.
- **Optional draft language:** (C) Not requested.
- **Confidence:** (B) High.

### Issue 2: Fees can rise without limit while the Customer is locked in

- **Where:** (A) Cl. 3: "may increase the fees once in each Contract Year on 30 days' notice." Cl. 11: "renews automatically for 12-month periods unless either party gives 90 days' notice."
- **What the contract says:** (A) The size of a fee increase is not limited. Termination for convenience is not in the provided text.
- **Inference:**
  - (B) An increase notified within the final 90 days of a term would take effect after the Customer's non-renewal window has closed.
  - (B) Combined with a 24-month initial term and no convenience exit, the Customer may have to pay an increase of any size.
  - (B) "Contract Year" is undefined, so the timing of the first permitted increase is uncertain.
- **Why it matters:** (B) The Customer's budget is exposed to uncapped increases it cannot escape.
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Cap annual increases, for example by reference to an index or a fixed percentage. Prohibit increases in the first 12 or 24 months. Require increase notices to be given before the non-renewal deadline. Alternatively, give the Customer a right to terminate on notice after any increase.
- **Optional draft language:** (C) Not requested.
- **Confidence:** (B) High.

### Issue 3: Supplier may use Customer Data for its own products

- **Where:** (A) Cl. 10: "Supplier may use Customer Data in aggregated form to improve its products."
- **What the contract says:** (A) Customer Data is "all data supplied by Customer to Supplier" (cl. 1). Cl. 10 contains no anonymisation standard, purpose limit beyond "improve its products", time limit or prohibition on benefiting third parties.
- **Inference:**
  - (B) "Aggregated" is undefined. It does not necessarily mean anonymised or non-identifying of the Customer.
  - (B) Retail sales and stock data is likely commercially sensitive. Improved Supplier products may serve the Customer's competitors.
  - (B) If Customer Data includes personal data, this use may raise data protection issues. That point is jurisdiction-dependent.
- **Why it matters:** (B) The Customer may be giving away competitive insight and taking on regulatory risk without compensation.
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Remove cl. 10. Alternatively, limit it to data that is anonymised, aggregated with other customers' data, and not identifying the Customer, its stores or individuals. Exclude personal data. Prohibit disclosure to third parties. End the right on termination.
- **Optional draft language:** (C) Not requested.
- **Confidence:** (B) Med.

### Issue 4: Weak data return and no deletion obligation on exit

- **Where:** (A) Cl. 11: "Supplier shall use reasonable efforts to return Customer Data on termination."
- **What the contract says:** (A) The cl. 11 obligation is "reasonable efforts" to return. It specifies no deadline, format or deletion duty, and it applies only "on termination".
- **Inference:**
  - (B) "Reasonable efforts" is a qualified obligation. Its precise strength is jurisdiction-dependent.
  - (B) "On termination" may not clearly cover expiry by non-renewal.
  - (B) Read with cl. 10, the Supplier may keep and continue using Customer Data after exit.
- **Why it matters:** (B) The Customer may struggle to migrate to another provider and cannot confirm its data has been purged.
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Make return an absolute obligation within a fixed period, in a usable format, on termination *or expiry*. Add certified deletion afterwards, subject only to legal retention requirements. Add reasonable exit assistance.
- **Optional draft language:** (C) Not requested.
- **Confidence:** (B) High.

### Issue 5: Exclusion of lost profit removes the Customer's main loss category

- **Where:** (A) Cl. 8: "Neither party is liable for loss of profit or indirect loss."
- **What the contract says:** (A) The exclusion is mutual. It is not expressly subject to the "Except for liability under clause 9" wording, which qualifies the cap sentence only.
- **Inference:**
  - (B) Losses from defective forecasts, such as lost sales, margin erosion or markdowns, may be characterised as loss of profit and excluded.
  - (B) The Customer's remaining recoverable loss may be limited to wasted fees and some direct costs.
  - (B) The exclusion may also reduce recovery under the cl. 9 indemnity if a third party's claim includes lost profits.
  - (B) How English law interprets "indirect loss" is jurisdiction-dependent.
- **Why it matters:** (B) The contract may leave the Customer with little practical remedy for the core failure it is buying protection against.
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Limit the exclusion to *indirect* loss of profit. Expressly list recoverable direct losses, such as wasted expenditure, costs of re-procurement and data restoration, and excess inventory costs. Exclude cl. 9 and data breaches from the exclusion.
- **Optional draft language:** (C) Not requested.
- **Confidence:** (B) Med.

### Issue 6: Service credits are the sole remedy for SLA failure

- **Where:** (A) Cl. 5: "Customer's sole remedy for any failure to meet a service level is the service credit in Exhibit B."
- **What the contract says:** (A) Exhibit B contains the credits and is not provided.
- **Inference:**
  - (B) "Sole remedy" may prevent damages claims for SLA failures.
  - (B) It may also be argued to prevent termination under cl. 11 for performance failures. Its interaction with cl. 11 is ambiguous.
  - (B) The value of the credits is unknown.
- **Why it matters:** (B) Chronic underperformance could cost the Customer only the credits, with no exit.
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Make credits a price adjustment that is "without prejudice" to other rights. Alternatively, keep credits as sole remedy only below a threshold. Add a termination right for repeated or serious SLA failures. Review Exhibit B before agreeing.
- **Optional draft language:** (C) Not requested.
- **Confidence:** (B) Med.

### Issue 7: Narrow performance warranty

- **Inference:**

  - (B) Whether statutory implied terms survive the exclusion is jurisdiction-dependent.
- **Why it matters:** (B) The warranty does not match the business plan, which is to obtain usable demand forecasts.
- **Proposed fix:** (C) Add warranties that the Services will:
  - (C) conform materially to Exhibit A;
  - (C) be performed by suitably qualified personnel in line with good industry practice;
  - (C) comply with applicable law.

  (C) Add a remedy of re-performance at no cost.

- **Confidence:** (B) Med.
