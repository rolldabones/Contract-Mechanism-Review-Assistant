## 5. Issue List (Prioritized)

### Issue 1 — Liability cap is low and may be near zero early in the term
- **Where:** Cl. 8: "each party's total liability under this Agreement shall not exceed the fees paid in the 6 months before the claim"
- **What the contract says (A):**
  - The cap is measured by fees *paid* in the prior 6 months.
  - The only carve-out is "liability under clause 9."
  - Loss of profit and indirect loss are excluded for both parties.
- **Inference (B):**
  - With 45-day payment terms (cl. 3), fees *paid* during the first months may be minimal. The cap could therefore be very low for an early claim.
  - "Total liability" combined with "the claim" is ambiguous. It may mean an aggregate cap fixed at the first claim, or a cap measured per claim.
  - There are no carve-outs for breach of confidentiality, data protection or security, wilful default, or Supplier's misuse of Customer Data.
  - The exclusion of "loss of profit" may cover the most likely loss from bad forecasts (lost sales, overstock or understock).
  - It is unclear whether the second sentence (loss-of-profit exclusion) also applies to cl. 9 indemnity claims. The "Except for…clause 9" wording appears to attach only to the first sentence.
  - **Jurisdiction-dependent:** Under English law, certain liabilities may not be excludable, and limitation clauses may be subject to statutory reasonableness controls. Please verify the position. I have not concluded on enforceability.
- **Why it matters:** For Customer, this is the main backstop for service failure and data incidents. As drafted, it may be disproportionate to the risk.
- **Proposed fix (C):**
  - Replace "paid in the 6 months" with fees "paid or payable in the 12 months," or a fixed floor amount, whichever is greater.
  - Clarify whether the cap is aggregate or per claim.
  - Add carve-outs, or a separate higher cap, for data protection, confidentiality and security breaches and for breach of cl. 10.
  - Carve out non-excludable liability.
  - Clarify that the cl. 9 indemnity is outside both sentences of cl. 8.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested. Available in Mode 3.
- **Confidence:** High (text) / Med (practical effect, which depends on Exhibit A fee profile)

### Issue 2 — Supplier's broad right to use Customer Data
- **Where:** Cl. 10: "Supplier may use Customer Data in aggregated form to improve its products"
- **What the contract says (A):** Permission to use Customer Data "in aggregated form." Customer Data is defined as "all data supplied by Customer to Supplier" (cl. 1).
- **Inference (B):**
  - "Aggregated" is undefined. Aggregated data is not necessarily anonymised.
  - The permission has no time limit, no stated post-termination limit, and no restriction on benefiting third parties, including Customer's competitors.
  - Retail sales and store data may include commercially sensitive information and possibly personal data. Whether personal data is in scope is unknown.
  - No data protection or processor terms appear in the provided text. **Jurisdiction-dependent:** UK data protection requirements may be relevant; please verify.
- **Why it matters:** This risks loss of competitive advantage and regulatory exposure. The permission also interacts with Issue 4 (no firm deletion obligation).
- **Proposed fix (C):**
  - Define "aggregated and anonymised" (non-identifiable as to Customer, its stores and individuals).
  - Exclude personal data.
  - Prohibit disclosure, and prohibit use specifically for Customer's competitors, if commercially achievable.
  - End the right on termination, or limit it to derived data that cannot be re-identified.
  - Alternatively, require Customer's prior written consent.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested.
- **Confidence:** High (text) / Med (degree of exposure, which depends on the data types)

### Issue 3 — Unilateral, uncapped fee increases

### Issue 4 — Weak data return; no deletion obligation
