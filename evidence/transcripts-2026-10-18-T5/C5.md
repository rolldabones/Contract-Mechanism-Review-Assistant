## 5. Issue List (Prioritized)

### Issue 1: Liability cap is low, ambiguous and lacks carve-outs
- **Where:** Cl. 8: "shall not exceed the fees paid in the 6 months before the claim"
- **(A) Fact:** The cap is set at fees *paid* in the prior 6 months. Only clause 9 liability is excepted. No other carve-outs appear in the provided text.
- **(B) Inference:**
  - (i) Early in the term, or if invoices are unpaid or disputed, "fees paid" could be very low.
  - (ii) "Total liability" is an aggregate, but it is measured against "the claim". With multiple claims, it is ambiguous which claim date fixes the cap.
  - (iii) The cap is mutual. It may be read to apply to Customer's liability generally. Whether it could affect Supplier's claim for unpaid fees is uncertain.
  - (iv) **Jurisdiction-dependent (England & Wales):** Certain liabilities may not be excludable as a matter of law, such as death or personal injury from negligence, and fraud. The clause's reasonableness may be tested under statute if it is on standard terms. Verify.
- **Why it matters:** The Customer's realistic losses, such as data breach, data misuse or failed service, could exceed 6 months' fees. There are no carve-outs for confidentiality, data protection or wilful misconduct.
- **Proposed fix:**
  - Raise the cap to at least 12 months' fees *paid or payable*, with a fixed reference point (e.g., the date of the first claim).
  - Add carve-outs, or a separate higher cap, for data-protection and confidentiality breaches, wilful misconduct and fraud.
  - Confirm that payment obligations sit outside the cap.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested. Available in Mode 3.
- **Confidence:** High (text). Medium (legal effect).

### Issue 2: Loss-of-profit exclusion undermines the deal's core value
- **Where:** Cl. 8: "Neither party is liable for loss of profit or indirect loss."
- **(A) Fact:** Lost profit and indirect loss are excluded for both parties. The "Except for liability under clause 9" wording precedes the first sentence.
- **(B) Inference:**
  - The likely Customer loss from defective forecasting, such as stock-outs or overstock, may be characterised as loss of profit and therefore be unrecoverable.
  - It is ambiguous whether the clause 9 exception also disapplies the loss-of-profit exclusion in the second sentence. If not, an IP indemnity claim could be reduced.
  - **Jurisdiction-dependent:** The meaning of "indirect loss" under English law has been the subject of case law and may be contested. Verify.
- **Why it matters:** It may leave Customer with no meaningful damages for the very failure mode the service exists to prevent.
- **Proposed fix:**
  - Clarify that the exclusions do not apply to clause 9 or to data or confidentiality breaches.
  - Consider defining recoverable direct losses, e.g., costs of re-procurement, data restoration, and wasted fees.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested.
- **Confidence:** High (text). Medium (inference).

### Issue 3: Broad, unlimited Supplier right to use Customer Data
- **Where:** Cl. 10: "may use Customer Data in aggregated form to improve its products"
- **(A) Fact:** "Aggregated" and "products" are undefined. The provided text has no anonymisation standard, no prohibition on re-identification, no confidentiality overlay and no end date.
- **(B) Inference:**
  - Retail sales and stock data may be commercially sensitive. "Improve its products" could include models offered to Customer's competitors.
  - Aggregation is not necessarily anonymisation.
  - **Jurisdiction-dependent:** If Customer Data includes personal data, UK data-protection law may require controller–processor terms and limit Supplier's own-purpose use. No such terms appear in the provided text. Verify.
- **Why it matters:** Competitive leakage, regulatory exposure and loss of control over a core asset.
- **Proposed fix:** Delete the clause, or narrow it so that:
  - data must be irreversibly anonymised and aggregated with other customers' data;
  - Customer is not identifiable;
  - personal data is excluded;
  - use is limited to improving the Services provided to Customer;
  - the right terminates on contract end.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested.
- **Confidence:** High (text). Medium (regulatory inference, pending confirmation of the data types).

### Issue 4: Weak data-return obligation; no deletion; outputs not covered
- **Where:** Cl. 11: "Supplier shall use reasonable efforts to return Customer Data on termination." Cl. 1: "data supplied by Customer"
- **(A) Fact:** The duty is "reasonable efforts" only. No timeline, format, deletion or certification is specified. It applies "on termination". Whether it applies on expiry or non-renewal is ambiguous.
- **(B) Inference:** Forecasts and derived outputs may not be "Customer Data". "Termination" may not clearly include natural expiry.
- **Why it matters:** Exit risk and operational continuity for store forecasting. Data remains with Supplier indefinitely.
- **Proposed fix:**
  - An absolute obligation to return all Customer Data and outputs within a set period, in a usable format.
  - Followed by deletion and written certification.
  - Applies on termination *or expiry*.
  - Consider exit-assistance provisions.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Issue 5: Uncapped annual fee increases interacting with auto-renewal
- **Where:** Cl. 3: "may increase the fees once in each Contract Year on 30 days' notice". Cl. 11: "renews automatically … unless either party gives 90 days' notice"

- **Why it matters:** Budget uncertainty and loss of leverage.
- **Proposed fix:**
  - Fix fees for the initial term.
  - Cap increases thereafter (e.g., at an index or a fixed percentage).

## 8. One-Page Summary

**Top 5 risks for Customer, with recommended positions (C):**

1. **Data reuse (cl. 10: "use Customer Data in aggregated form to improve its products").** Delete, or limit to anonymised, non-identifying use for Customer's own Services, ending on termination.
2. **Low liability cap and lost-profit exclusion (cl. 8: "fees paid in the 6 months before the claim"; "loss of profit").** Seek a 12-month (or higher) cap on fees paid or payable, a super-cap or carve-out for data and confidentiality breaches, and clarification that the exclusions do not apply to the indemnity.
3. **Exit and data return (cl. 11: "reasonable efforts to return Customer Data").** Seek an absolute obligation to return and delete, with a timeline, covering outputs, and applying on expiry as well as termination.
4. **Price escalation and auto-renewal (cl. 3: "once in each Contract Year on 30 days' notice"; cl. 11: "90 days' notice").** Seek fixed fees for the initial term, an indexed cap thereafter, and increase notices timed before the renewal deadline or a termination right.
5. **Thin performance protection (cl. 5: "sole remedy"; cl. 7: "reasonable skill and care").** Seek a chronic-failure termination right and a warranty of conformance to Exhibit A. Review Exhibit B before finalising.
