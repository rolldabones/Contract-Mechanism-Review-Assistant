## 5. Issue List (Prioritized)

### Issue 1: Liability cap is low and look-back-based; lost profit is excluded; carve-outs are narrow
- **Where:** Cl. 8: "shall not exceed the fees paid in the 6 months before the claim"; "Neither party is liable for loss of profit or indirect loss"; "Except for liability under clause 9."
- **What the contract says:**
  - The cap is mutual and equals fees *paid* in the 6 months before "the claim."
  - Lost profit and indirect loss are excluded.
  - The only carve-out is cl. 9.
- **Inference:**
  - (i) Early in the term, before much is paid, the cap could be near zero.
  - (ii) The cap is tied to "fees paid," so it could shrink if Customer is withholding disputed payments.
  - (iii) With multiple claims, it is unclear which "claim" date sets the look-back, and whether the cap is per claim or aggregate. The word "total" suggests aggregate.
  - (iv) There is no carve-out for data/confidentiality breach, data protection breach, wilful misconduct or fraud.
  - (v) It is ambiguous whether "total liability" could be read to cap Customer's own fee payment obligation. This is likely a point Supplier would want to clarify.
- **Why it matters:** For a forecasting product, Customer's realistic losses are likely to be profit-type losses, so Customer's practical remedy may be minimal.
- **Jurisdiction flag:** Under English law, some liabilities cannot be excluded, such as death or personal injury from negligence, and fraud. Limitation clauses may also be subject to a reasonableness test in some circumstances, such as standard terms under UCTA 1977. The meaning of "indirect loss" depends on English case law. These are for the lawyer to verify. No enforceability conclusion is drawn here.
- **Proposed fix (plain English):**
  - Raise the cap to a higher multiple, for example 12 months' fees *payable* (not paid), with a minimum floor amount.
  - Add carve-outs for confidentiality, data protection and security breaches, wilful misconduct, fraud, and liabilities that cannot be limited by law.
  - Consider a separate, higher cap for data breaches.
  - Clarify that the cap does not apply to fees due.
  - Consider defining recoverable heads of loss, such as costs of procuring replacement services and data restoration costs.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested.
- **Confidence:** High on text; Medium on practical impact (depends on fee levels in Exhibit A).

### Issue 2: Broad Supplier right to use Customer Data
- **Where:** Cl. 10: "Supplier may use Customer Data in aggregated form to improve its products."
- **What the contract says:** Supplier has a right to use Customer Data, limited only by "aggregated form" and the purpose "to improve its products." No duration, anonymisation standard or exclusion of personal data is stated in the provided text.
- **Inference:**
  - Retail sales and inventory data may be commercially sensitive.
  - "Aggregated" is undefined. It could cover data aggregated only within Northmere's own dataset, which may not prevent identification of Northmere's patterns.
  - "Improve its products" could benefit Supplier's other customers, possibly including Customer's competitors.
  - The right appears to continue after termination, since no limit is stated.
- **Why it matters:** Risk of competitive leakage and loss of control over data. If personal data is included, there may also be data protection exposure (jurisdiction-dependent, e.g. UK GDPR purpose limitation and controller/processor roles).
- **Proposed fix:**
  - Define "aggregated" to require anonymisation and combination with other customers' data so that neither Customer nor any individual is identifiable.
  - Exclude personal data.
  - Prohibit disclosure or sale to third parties.
  - Limit use to the term, or require deletion on exit.
  - Alternatively, delete cl. 10 or make it opt-in.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Issue 3: Uncapped annual fee increases on short notice, interacting with auto-renewal
- **Where:** Cl. 3: "Supplier may increase the fees once in each Contract Year on 30 days' notice." Cl. 11: "renews automatically…unless either party gives 90 days' notice."
- **What the contract says:** One increase per Contract Year is allowed with 30 days' notice, and no ceiling is stated. "Contract Year" is undefined in the provided text.
- **Inference:**
  - The increase right appears to apply during the initial 24-month term as well, which reduces price certainty in the committed period.
  - The 30-day notice is shorter than the 90-day non-renewal window, so Customer may be locked in before learning of a renewal-year increase.
- **Why it matters:** Budget uncertainty and loss of leverage at renewal.
- **Proposed fix:**
  - Fix fees for the initial term.
  - Cap any increase at an index (e.g. CPI) or a fixed percentage.
  - Require increase notice at least 30 days *before* the non-renewal deadline (i.e. 120+ days before renewal).
  - Or give Customer a right to terminate without penalty on receiving an increase notice.

- **Confidence:** High.

## 6. Negotiation Package

**Must have**
1. A higher liability cap based on fees *payable*, plus carve-outs for data, confidentiality and security breach, wilful misconduct and fraud (Issue 1).
2. Restricted data use: anonymised and pooled, no personal data, no third-party disclosure, deletion at exit (Issue 2).
3. A fee increase cap and notice aligned to the renewal window, or a termination right on increase (Issue 3).
4. Firm data return and deletion on termination or expiry (Issue 7).
5. Sight of Exhibits A and B, cl. 12.3 and the full agreement before signature (Issues 4, 6, 9).

**Nice to have**
- Termination for persistent SLA failure, and confirmation that credits do not exclude termination (Issue 4).
- A conformance warranty and other warranties (Issue 5).
- A "Deliverables" definition and a broader indemnity (Issue 6).
- Insolvency termination, shorter renewal notice and transition assistance (Issue 8).
- A disputed-invoice mechanism (Issue 10).

**Can concede**
- The mutual structure of the cap, if the level and carve-outs improve. (B) A mutual cap also limits Customer's own exposure.
- The 24-month initial term, if price is fixed for that term and exit rights are added.
- Governing law and venue (cl. 14). (B) These appear consistent with a UK plc customer, but the lawyer should confirm.
- The lost-profit exclusion in general form, if specific recoverable heads of loss (e.g. re-procurement costs) are expressly preserved.

**Suggested trading strategy (B)**
- Lead with the data and liability package (Issues 1, 2, 7). These are typically framed as governance and compliance necessities, which may make them harder for Supplier to resist.
- Offer price certainty in return for term commitment: accept 24 months in exchange for fixed fees in the initial term and capped increases thereafter.
- Treat the SLA, warranty and termination items as a bundle. Concede some warranty scope if a persistent-failure exit right is granted.
- Defer final positions on Issues 4 and 6 until Exhibit B and cl. 12.3 are reviewed.
