## 5. Issue List (Prioritized)

### Issue 1: Low liability cap with narrow carve-outs
- **Where:** Cl. 8: *"total liability under this Agreement shall not exceed the fees paid in the 6 months before the claim."*
- **(A) Fact:**
  - Mutual cap of fees **paid** in the 6 months before "the claim."
  - The only carve-out is cl. 9.
  - No carve-outs for data protection, confidentiality, cl. 10 misuse, fraud, or death/personal injury.
- **(B) Inference:**
  - Early in the term, before payment cycles complete (45-day terms, cl. 3), fees "paid" may be low or zero.
  - The phrase "before the claim" is ambiguous. It could mean before the event or before notification, and it could operate per claim or in aggregate (the wording says "total").
  - Under English law, some liabilities cannot be excluded or limited (e.g., death/personal injury from negligence, fraud). The clause's failure to say so may raise construction or reasonableness questions. **This is jurisdiction-dependent; verify.**
- **Why it matters:** A data breach or a significant service failure could cause the Customer losses far above 6 months' fees.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**
  - Raise the cap to the greater of 12 months' fees **paid or payable** and a fixed floor.
  - Define the reference period by the date of the event giving rise to the claim.
  - Add carve-outs, or a separate higher cap, for: data protection breach, confidentiality breach, breach of cl. 10, fraud, wilful default, and death/personal injury.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested. Available in Mode 3.
- **Confidence:** High (text). Medium (English-law consequences).

### Issue 2: Exclusion of loss of profit and indirect loss defeats the Customer's core loss
- **Where:** Cl. 8: *"Neither party is liable for loss of profit or indirect loss."*
- **(A) Fact:**
  - There is a blanket exclusion of loss of profit and indirect loss.
  - The exception *"Except for liability under clause 9"* appears in the first sentence only.
- **(B) Inference:**
  - The likely loss from defective forecasting is lost sales, wasted stock and markdowns. Much of this may be characterised as loss of profit.
  - It is unclear whether the cl. 9 exception also disapplies the second sentence. If it does not, the IP indemnity may not cover a third party's lost-profit claim against the Customer.
  - "Indirect loss" is undefined. English courts have historically tended to read it by reference to the second limb of *Hadley v Baxendale*, but recent authority has questioned that approach. **Jurisdiction-dependent; verify current position.**
- **Why it matters:** Combined with the cap, the Customer's realistic recovery for a failed service may be close to nil.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**
  - Make clear that the cl. 9 exception applies to the whole of cl. 8.
  - Carve specified recoverable losses out of the exclusion, e.g.:
    - costs of procuring replacement services;
    - wasted expenditure;
    - costs of data restoration;
    - regulatory fines where lawfully recoverable;
    - remediation costs.
  - Define "indirect loss."
- **Confidence:** High (text). Medium (characterisation).

### Issue 3: Broad Supplier right to use Customer Data
- **Where:** Cl. 10: *"Supplier may use Customer Data in aggregated form to improve its products."* Cl. 1: *"all data supplied by Customer."*
- **(A) Fact:**
  - The right is not limited by time, purpose beyond "improve its products," anonymisation, confidentiality, or a bar on benefiting third parties.
  - "Aggregated form" is undefined.
- **(B) Inference:**
  - "Aggregated" is not the same as anonymised. Aggregated sales data may still be commercially sensitive.
  - Products improved with Northmere data may be sold to competitors.
  - If personal data is included, this use may require a lawful basis and appropriate controller/processor terms under UK data protection law. **Jurisdiction-dependent.**
  - The use right appears to survive termination by default, since there is no limit.
- **Why it matters:** This risks loss of commercial confidentiality and regulatory exposure. It also interacts with Issue 1, because there is no carve-out from the cap.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**
  - Delete cl. 10, or restrict it to data that is irreversibly anonymised and not identifiable to Northmere, its stores or its customers.
  - Exclude personal data.
  - Prohibit disclosure to, or use for the benefit of, identified competitors.
  - End the right on termination.
  - Require a data processing agreement.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Issue 4: Uncapped annual fee increases combined with auto-renewal
- **Where:** Cl. 3: *"may increase the fees once in each Contract Year on 30 days' notice."* Cl. 11: *"renews automatically… unless either party gives 90 days' notice."*
- **(A) Fact:**
  - There is no cap, index or justification requirement.
  - There is no Customer termination right on an increase.
  - "Contract Year" is undefined.
- **(B) Inference:**
  - The increase right appears to apply during the 24-month initial term as well as renewals, which undermines budget certainty.
  - The timing gap described in Section 4 can lock the Customer into a renewal at an increased price.
- **Why it matters:** The Customer cannot control price over the term.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**
  - Fix fees for the initial term.
  - After that, cap increases (e.g., at a stated index or percentage).
  - Require at least 120 days' notice, so the notice arrives before the non-renewal deadline.
  - Give the Customer a right to terminate without penalty on any increase.
  - Define "Contract Year."
- **Confidence:** High.

### Issue 5: Service credits as sole remedy; Exhibit B unknown
- **Where:** Cl. 5: *"Customer's sole remedy for any failure to meet a service level is the service credit in Exhibit B."*
- **(A) Fact:** The credit is the exclusive remedy for SLA failure. Exhibit B was not provided.
- **(B) Inference:**
  - It is unclear whether "sole remedy" prevents the Customer from treating persistent SLA failure as a "material breach" under cl. 11. English courts construe exclusive-remedy wording on its terms. **Jurisdiction-dependent.**
  - Credit amounts may be nominal.
- **Why it matters:** The Customer may be stuck with a chronically underperforming service.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**
  - State that credits are without prejudice to termination rights.
  - Add a chronic-failure termination trigger (e.g., X misses in Y months).
  - Specify that credits are a price adjustment, not the sole remedy for losses beyond the SLA failure itself.
  - Review Exhibit B before concluding.
- **Confidence:** High (text). Low (overall effect, given the missing exhibit).

### Issue 6: Weak data return; no deletion or transition obligation
- **Where:** Cl. 11: *"Supplier shall use reasonable efforts to return Customer Data on termination."*
- **(A) Fact:**
  - This is an efforts-based obligation.
  - It does not state format, timeframe, deletion, certification or exit assistance.
  - It refers only to "termination," not expiry.
- **(B) Inference:**
  - "Termination" may not clearly capture expiry by non-renewal.
  - Combined with cl. 10, the Supplier could retain data indefinitely.
- **Why it matters:** This creates operational continuity risk (replacing the forecasting tool) and data-protection risk.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**
  - Make return an absolute obligation, within X days, in a stated machine-readable format, on termination **or expiry**.
  - Follow it with deletion and written certification.
  - Add reasonable exit assistance.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Issue 7: Thin warranty; no conformity or acceptance standard
- **Where:** Cl. 7: *"performed with reasonable skill and care. All other warranties are excluded to the extent permitted by law."*
- **(A) Fact:**
  - There is only a skill-and-care warranty.
  - There is no warranty of conformity with Exhibit A, no acceptance process, and no remedy such as re-performance.
- **(B) Inference:**
  - The exclusion likely removes implied terms beyond reasonable care.
  - Whether it is effective may depend on statutory controls on exclusion clauses (e.g., reasonableness requirements in B2B contracts under English law). **Jurisdiction-dependent; verify.**
- **Why it matters:** The Customer has no contractual assurance that the service will do what Exhibit A describes.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**
  - Add warranties that:
    - the Services will conform materially to Exhibit A;
    - the Supplier will comply with applicable law;
    - Deliverables will not contain malicious code.
  - Add a re-performance or refund remedy.
  - Add acceptance criteria for initial implementation.
- **Confidence:** High (text). Medium (legal effect).

  - "Deliverables" is not defined in the provided text.

**Can concede**

- Retaining auto-renewal, if the notice period is shortened and fee-increase timing is aligned.

**Suggested trading strategy**

- **Offer payment-term flexibility and auto-renewal tolerance** in exchange for price certainty (Issue 4).
