## 5. Issue List (Prioritised)

### Issue 1: Liability cap is low and the lost-profit exclusion undercuts the deal's value
- **Where:** Cl. 8: *"shall not exceed the fees paid in the 6 months before the claim. Neither party is liable for loss of profit or indirect loss."*
- **(A) Fact:**
  - The cap is mutual and limited to fees *paid* in the 6 months before "the claim."
  - Liability under cl. 9 is excepted from the cap.
  - Loss of profit and indirect loss are excluded for both parties.
  - The text contains no carve-outs for confidentiality, data breach, fraud, wilful misconduct, or death or personal injury.
- **(B) Inference:**
  - Early in the term, or if fees are paid in arrears, "fees paid" could be very small.
  - "The claim" is ambiguous where there are several claims.
  - It is unclear whether the "Except for liability under clause 9" carve-out applies only to the cap (first sentence) or also to the lost-profit exclusion (second sentence). If it applies only to the cap, IP indemnity recoveries could still be reduced by the exclusion.
  - Some exclusions may be restricted by law, but enforceability is not concluded here. This is jurisdiction-dependent (for example, statutory controls on excluding certain liabilities).
- **Why it matters:** Customer's most likely losses (data misuse, bad forecasts leading to lost margin) are either capped low or excluded.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**
  - Raise the cap to the greater of 12 months' fees *paid or payable* and a fixed sum.
  - Add a higher or uncapped tier for data protection, confidentiality and security breaches.
  - Add standard carve-outs (fraud, wilful misconduct, death or personal injury from negligence).
  - Clarify that the cl. 9 carve-out applies to both sentences of cl. 8.
  - Narrow the lost-profit exclusion to *indirect* lost profit, or list recoverable direct losses.
- **Optional draft language:** Not requested; available in Mode 3.
- **Confidence:** High on the text. Medium on the interpretation of the carve-out scope.

### Issue 2: Customer Data reuse licence is unrestricted
- **Where:** Cl. 10: *"Supplier may use Customer Data in aggregated form to improve its products."*
- **(A) Fact:** "Aggregated" is undefined. The clause contains no anonymisation standard, no ban on identifying Customer, no ban on disclosure to third parties or competitors, no duration limit and no opt-out. "Customer Data" covers *"all data supplied by Customer"* (cl. 1).
- **(B) Inference:**
  - Customer sales and stock data is likely to be commercially sensitive. Aggregated insights could benefit competing retailers who use the same Supplier.
  - If Customer Data includes personal data (for example, loyalty data), data-protection law may apply. This is jurisdiction-dependent, and no processing terms appear in the provided text.
  - The licence appears to continue after termination, since nothing in the text ends it.
- **Why it matters:** This creates confidentiality, competitive and regulatory exposure, with no remedy in the provided text.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**
  - Delete cl. 10.
  - If it must stay: limit it to irreversibly anonymised data that is aggregated with other customers' data and not attributable to Customer. Exclude personal data. Prohibit disclosure of Customer-identifiable insights. Make use subject to confidentiality. End the licence on termination.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Issue 3: SLA "sole remedy" may block termination or damages for chronic failure
- **Where:** Cl. 5: *"Customer's sole remedy for any failure to meet a service level is the service credit in Exhibit B."*
- **(A) Fact:** Credits are the sole remedy for SLA failure. The credit amounts and service levels are in Exhibit B, which was not provided.
- **(B) Inference:** "Sole remedy" could be argued to exclude termination under cl. 11 for repeated SLA misses. Credits may be capped or immaterial; this is unknown without Exhibit B.
- **Why it matters:** Customer could be locked into a 24-month term with a persistently underperforming service.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**
  - Make credits a price adjustment and not a sole remedy, or keep the sole-remedy wording but add exceptions: a chronic-failure termination right (for example, X misses in Y months), and damages where the failure is also a breach of cl. 7.
  - Obtain and review Exhibit B.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The analysis depends on Exhibit B.

### Issue 4: Uncapped unilateral fee increases during a fixed term
- **Where:** Cl. 3: *"Supplier may increase the fees once in each Contract Year on 30 days' notice."*
- **(A) Fact:** No cap, index or Customer termination right is in the provided text. "Contract Year" is undefined in the provided text.
- **(B) Inference:** The fixed 24-month term plus auto-renewal (cl. 11) leaves Customer little room to respond to an increase.
- **Why it matters:** This is budget risk, and it undermines the business case.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**
  - Fix fees for the initial term.
  - Cap later increases at the lower of CPI (or a specified index) and X%.
  - Require at least 90 days' notice, aligned with the renewal notice window.
  - Give Customer a right to terminate without penalty if an increase exceeds the cap.
  - Define "Contract Year."
- **Confidence:** High.

### Issue 5: Narrow warranty does not cover output conformity
- **Where:** Cl. 7: *"performed with reasonable skill and care. All other warranties are excluded to the extent permitted by law."*
- **(A) Fact:** The only express warranty concerns the standard of performance. There is no warranty that the Services conform to Exhibit A, that they meet any accuracy or methodology standard, or that they are free of malicious code.
- **(B) Inference:** Implied terms may be excluded only to the extent the law allows (jurisdiction-dependent; enforceability not concluded). Customer would need to prove a lack of reasonable care rather than simply show non-conformity.
- **Why it matters:** This raises Customer's burden of proof for defective output.
- **(C) Proposed fix:** Add warranties that:
  - the Services conform materially to Exhibit A;
  - the agreed forecasting methodology is used;
  - Supplier complies with applicable law;
  - there is no material degradation of functionality during the term.
- **Confidence:** High on the text. Low on the effect of the implied-term exclusion, which is jurisdiction-dependent.

### Issue 6: Weak post-termination data return
- **Where:** Cl. 11: *"Supplier shall use reasonable efforts to return Customer Data on termination."*
- **(A) Fact:** This is an efforts-based obligation. It has no timeframe, format, deletion obligation or certification. Return of forecasts or outputs is not mentioned.
- **(B) Inference:** Customer may be unable to migrate to a replacement provider promptly.
- **Why it matters:** Exit and continuity risk. Combined with cl. 10, Supplier may keep and use data indefinitely.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**
  - Make return an absolute obligation, within X days, in a stated machine-readable format.
  - Require secure deletion afterwards, with written certification.
  - Add transition assistance at agreed rates.
- **Confidence:** High.

- **Why it matters:** Indemnity value is unknowable as provided.
- **(C) Proposed fix:**

## 6. Negotiation Package (C: Strategy)

**Must have**
- Cl. 10 data-use restriction or deletion (Issue 2).
- Higher cap plus a data/confidentiality super-cap, and clarified carve-outs (Issue 1).
- Chronic-SLA termination right, and credits not the sole remedy for related warranty breach (Issue 3).
- Absolute data return and deletion obligation (Issue 6).
- Sight of Exhibits A and B, cl. 12.3 and the full agreement (Issues 7 and 9).

**Nice to have**
- Fee cap and an exit right on increase (Issue 4). This could move to must-have depending on budget sensitivity.
- Conformity and methodology warranties (Issue 5).
- Termination for convenience (Issue 8).
- Invoice-dispute mechanism (Issue 10).

**Can concede**
- Mutual application of the cap, since it also protects Customer.
- Mutual exclusion of *indirect* loss, if direct lost margin is carved back in.
- The 30-day cure period.
- London courts (cl. 14), which appear workable for a UK plc. Confirm commercially.
- Once-per-year fee review, if it is capped and indexed.

**Suggested trading strategy**
- Lead with data (cl. 10) and the liability cap. These are the issues hardest to justify for a retail data customer, and the ones most likely to matter to Customer's board and DPO.
- Offer to accept Supplier's fee-review right (capped and indexed) and a longer commitment in exchange for data restrictions and a higher cap.
- If Supplier insists on the SLA sole remedy, trade it for a meaningful chronic-failure exit and credit levels verified in Exhibit B.
- Make the IP indemnity position conditional on seeing cl. 12.3 before agreeing to any wording.
